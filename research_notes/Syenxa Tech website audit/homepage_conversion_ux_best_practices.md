# Home page best practices for a small B2B AI-automation agency (conversion, UX, accessibility, performance, search/AI-search) — evidence as of October 2026

Reader notes on evidence quality (applies to every section):
- "PRIMARY" = I read the original source this session. "SECONDARY" = figure reached me through a write-up of the original; verify before quoting in a client-facing document.
- "VENDOR" = published by a company that sells a related product; methodology is often undisclosed or correlational.
- "OLD" = study predates 2024.
- Nothing here is legal advice; the FTC and ADA items describe published rule text, agency guidance and filing counts.

## 1. Above the fold: headline clarity, value-proposition specificity, number of CTAs, hero imagery vs demonstration, speed of judgement

### Takeaway
Visitors form an aesthetic impression in well under a second and decide whether to stay within roughly 10 seconds, and most viewing time is still spent in the first one to two screenfuls, so the hero must state in plain words what the company does, for whom, and offer one dominant action. Simple, familiar layouts, plain-language copy and real/product imagery outperform visually complex, jargon-heavy, stock-image heroes in the available evidence, although most CTA-count and copy data are correlational.

### Cited Findings
- PRIMARY, OLD (2018): NN/g eyetracking (120 participants, 130,000+ fixations, 1920x1080) found users spent about 57% of page-viewing time above the fold (down from 80% in 2010), 74% in the first two screenfuls and 81% in the first three; over 65% of above-the-fold viewing time was in the top half of the viewport. NN/g's recommendation: "Reserve the top of the page for high-priority content", keep major CTAs above the fold, and avoid "false floors" that hide the fact that more content exists below. — [NN/g, Scrolling and Attention](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/)
- SECONDARY summary of NN/g, OLD: the average page visit lasts a little under a minute; "the first 10 seconds of the page visit are critical for users' decision to stay or leave"; users remain highly likely to leave during the next 20 seconds and the drop-off only slows after about 30 seconds (based on a Microsoft Research dwell-time analysis). — [NN/g, How Long Do Users Stay on Web Pages](https://nngroup.com/articles/how-long-do-users-stay-on-web-pages/)
- OLD (2006), SECONDARY: Lindgaard et al. (Carleton University) found visual-appeal ratings given after a 50 ms exposure correlated well with ratings after longer exposure; only the third of three studies used 50 ms, and Lindgaard cautioned the data speak to the speed of an aesthetic reaction, not to a reasoned judgement of content. — [WebsiteOptimization.com summary](https://www.websiteoptimization.com/speed/tweak/blink/)
- OLD (2012): Tuch et al. (University of Basel with a Google/YouTube UX co-author; 119 real website screenshots) found visual complexity and prototypicality affect aesthetic judgement within 17 ms; sites with low visual complexity and high prototypicality (looking like what users expect for the category) were rated most appealing. This is an aesthetics measure, not a trust or conversion measure. — [Google Research publication page](https://research.google/pubs/pub38315/)
- OLD: NN/g homepage guidelines advise starting with a one-sentence tagline that says what the company does, which matters most for newer or less famous brands. — [NN/g, Top Ten Guidelines for Homepage Usability](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/top-ten-guidelines-for-homepage-usability/)
- OLD: NN/g eyetracking found that large decorative "feel-good" images are completely ignored, while photos of products and real people are looked at; generic stock photos of people are ignored. — [NN/g, Photos as Web Content](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/photos-as-web-content/)
- VENDOR, SECONDARY (2024): Unbounce's Conversion Benchmark Report put the cross-industry median landing-page conversion rate at 6.6%; copy written at a 5th-7th grade reading level converted best (11.1%, reported as 56% better than the comparison group), and the negative correlation between "difficult words" (3+ syllables) and conversion was reported as 62% stronger than in 2020. — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/how-does-your-landing-page-performance-compare-453224); [DMN coverage](https://dmn.ca/unbounces-2024-conversion-benchmark-report-proves-that-attention-spans-are-declining-and-so-are-conversion-rates/); method at [Unbounce methodology](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/methodology/)
- VENDOR, OLD, SECONDARY, correlational: an Unbounce analysis of 18,639 landing pages reported 13.5% average conversion for pages with one link/CTA vs 11.9% with two and 10.5% with three or more; pages with 1MB+ images converted lower; pages with 800+ words had a 33% lower median conversion than pages under 200 words. These are campaign landing pages, not home pages. — [MarketingProfs 2018](https://www.marketingprofs.com/charts/2018/33935/what-converts-web-landing-page-benchmarks-and-trends-infographic); [MarketingProfs 2017](https://www.marketingprofs.com/charts/2017/31924/lead-gen-landing-page-benchmarks-conversion-rates-and-content-metrics)
- Single-organisation A/B test (non-profit, not B2B): adding explicit value-proposition copy to a home page raised conversion from 4.8% to 5.9% (+24.7% relative). — [NextAfter experiment](https://www.nextafter.com/?p=16473)

### Inferences
- Requirement candidates: (a) an H1 that names the service category and the audience in plain words (target roughly 6th-8th grade reading level, no three-syllable jargon stacks); (b) a one-sentence sub-headline with a concrete outcome; (c) one primary CTA and at most one visually subordinate secondary CTA in the hero; (d) the primary CTA and the first proof element visible without scrolling at common laptop and phone viewports; (e) a visible cue that content continues below the fold.
- For an AI calling/chatbot agency, a hero that shows the product working (a call transcript, a chat exchange, a short demo) is better supported than abstract or decorative imagery, because NN/g found decorative images are ignored and product imagery is examined; this is an extrapolation from general eyetracking, not a tested B2B-agency result.
- A conventional layout (logo top-left, nav top, headline left, CTA button) is favoured by the prototypicality finding; highly unconventional hero layouts carry a first-impression cost.

### Gaps
- No rigorous 2024-2026 study isolating "number of hero CTAs" on B2B service home pages; the Unbounce CTA-count figures are old, correlational and landing-page specific.
- I could not retrieve CXL's own home-page value-proposition test results or any Baymard B2B-services home-page research (Baymard's published work is predominantly e-commerce).
- The NN/g scrolling data is from 2018 on desktop monitors; no newer NN/g replication for mobile was found.

## 2. Page structure: section order, long vs short, sticky CTAs, lead forms and booking calendars

### Takeaway
There is no validated universal section order; what the evidence supports is front-loading (what you do, proof, primary CTA in the first two screenfuls), giving buyers enough self-serve detail to shortlist without talking to sales, and keeping the conversion action reachable throughout a long page. Form length matters less than what the fields ask for (phone, address and free-text areas hurt most); calendar-vs-form evidence is vendor-only.

### Cited Findings
- PRIMARY press release (survey of 632 B2B buyers, fielded Aug-Sep 2024, published June 2025): 61% of B2B buyers prefer an overall rep-free buying experience; 73% actively avoid suppliers who send irrelevant outreach; buyers prefer self-service for general learning but seller input for judging fit with their own company. — [Gartner press release](https://gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience)
- VENDOR (nearly 4,000 B2B buyers, 2025): first contact with a vendor happens about 61% of the way through the buying journey (69% in 2024); buyers initiate contact themselves over 80% of the time; the winning vendor is on the "Day One" shortlist 95% of the time, and about four in five deals go to the pre-contact favourite. Sample skews to larger software purchases with roughly 10-month cycles, not SMB services. — [6sense Buyer Experience Report](https://6sense.com/report/buyer-experience/); [Business Wire release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251112018032/en)
- PRIMARY, OLD (2018): 74% of viewing time falls in the first two screenfuls and 81% in the first three; attention drops sharply after that, so page length is tolerated but lower sections get a small share of attention. — [NN/g, Scrolling and Attention](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/)
- Long vs short is unresolved: Unbounce's own review concludes both camps are right and the ideal length depends on the goal of the page; individual tests go both ways (shorter page +54% for a free signup; longer pages winning for higher-consideration offers). — [Unbounce, Size Matters](https://unbounce.com/?p=20484)
- VENDOR case studies (publication-biased, single sites): a sticky header raised paid subscriptions by 13% on Mobbin; a home-page sticky CTA test reported +20.4% conversion with 95.1% probability to beat control on 25,935 visitors and 327 orders (small order count). — [Conversion Rate Experts](https://conversion-rate-experts.com/mobbin-sticky-header-win-report/); [Convertibles case study](https://convertibles.dev/blogs/case-studies/homepage-sticky-cta-case-study)
- VENDOR, OLD, correlational (40,000+ HubSpot customer landing pages): conversion declines only slightly as field count rises; single-line text fields cost little, multiple text areas depress conversion strongly; asking for a phone number was associated with conversion falling from 19% to 13%, street address 16% to 12%, age 18% to 15%. — [HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/blog/tabid/6307/bid/6746/which-types-of-form-fields-lower-landing-page-conversions.aspx); [SmartBug summary](https://smartbugmedia.com/blog/landing-page-best-practices-how-many-form-fields)
- VENDOR, unverified at source: multi-step forms are reported at 13.9% conversion vs 4.5% for single-page forms, attributed to Formstack; other write-ups attribute different numbers (14% higher; "up to 300%") to the same vendor, so the figure is unreliable. — [Reform.app](https://reform.app/blog/single-page-vs-multi-step-forms-which-converts-better)
- VENDOR, no disclosed method or control group: Chili Piper claims customers go from converting about 50% to 80% of inbound leads into pipeline with instant scheduling, and cites single-customer results (e.g. meetings set +71%). — [Chili Piper](https://www.chilipiper.com/solutions/sales)
- OLD: NN/g found that B2B buyers building a shortlist need "only a general idea of cost levels" and that the site's job at that stage is to "survive the pruning process"; simple tables of sample prices beat complex calculators for early research. — [NN/g, Show Prices for Common Scenarios](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/show-prices-for-common-scenarios/)

### Inferences
- A defensible order, derived from attention decay plus buyer self-service preference (not from a controlled test of section order): 1) hero with what/for whom/outcome + primary CTA; 2) immediate proof strip (verifiable only); 3) problem and outcome framing; 4) the four services with one-line outcomes and links to service pages; 5) demonstration or explanatory visual; 6) how it works / process with timeline; 7) fuller proof (case evidence, named people); 8) pricing signal ("from" prices or sample scenarios); 9) FAQ; 10) final CTA with low-friction booking. Items 1-2 must fit in the first two screenfuls.
- Because most viewing time is early and buyers shortlist before contact, a long home page is acceptable only if each section is scannable and a persistent CTA (sticky header button on desktop, bottom bar or header button on mobile) keeps the action reachable.
- Lead capture: offer both a short form (name, work email, one optional free-text field; no mandatory phone or address) and direct calendar booking, and A/B test locally; no independent evidence says which wins.

### Gaps
- No independent, large-sample study of section order on B2B agency/service home pages was found; published "high-converting structure" advice is agency opinion.
- No independent controlled comparison of embedded booking calendar vs contact form (vendor claims only).
- Sticky-CTA evidence is from e-commerce/SaaS case studies with small samples; none is specific to B2B service lead generation.
- Form-field evidence is 10+ years old and correlational; I found no recent large-sample replication.

## 3. Trust for a new/small agency without big-name logos; fake or illustrative testimonials and FTC 16 CFR Part 465

### Takeaway
Buyers trust peers and third-party-verifiable evidence far more than vendor claims, and buyer trust in online information is falling, so a small agency should rely on proof a sceptic can check (named people, linked third-party reviews, specific and sourced numbers, visible founders, sample pricing, a documented process) rather than volume of praise. Invented or "illustrative" testimonials presented as real are squarely the conduct the FTC's Consumer Review Rule targets (writing or creating a testimonial that misrepresents that the testimonialist exists or had the experience), with civil penalties of up to $53,088 per violation; the only safe forms are real, attributable testimonials or content that is unmistakably labelled as hypothetical.

### Cited Findings
- SurveyMonkey + Reddit study (March 2026, 1,200 US decision-makers): 73% trust peer recommendations, vs vendor websites 55%, search engines 54%, review sites 46%, AI chatbots 39%, social media 36%; 55% say it is difficult to identify reliable information sources and almost half struggle to find genuine user testimonials; real-user testimonials rated "very valuable" by 37%. — [Demand Gen Report](https://www.demandgenreport.com/industry-news/news-brief/trust-is-the-new-currency-in-b2b-buying-surveymonkey-reddit/52167/)
- VENDOR (TrustRadius 2026 B2B Buying Disconnect, 1,862 buyers and 444 vendors; software buyers): share of buyers who trust online resources less than before rose from 39% to 47% in a year; 74% consulted customer reviews; analyst reports used by only 13%; demos, free trials, prior experience and user reviews ranked most influential. The 2025 edition found 54% of buyers speak with an existing user before buying (vendors guessed 38%). — [Demand Gen Report on TrustRadius 2026](https://www.demandgenreport.com/?p=53853); [TrustRadius media kit](https://solutions.trustradius.com/bridging-the-trust-gap-media-kit/)
- OLD framework, re-validated by NN/g: four ways a site communicates trustworthiness are design quality, up-front disclosure, comprehensive and current content, and connection to the rest of the web. — [NN/g, Trustworthy Design](https://nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/)
- OLD: NN/g calls missing prices on B2B sites a top design mistake and recommends sample prices for typical orders when exact pricing varies. — [NN/g, Show Prices for Common Scenarios](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/show-prices-for-common-scenarios/)
- PRIMARY rule text: 16 CFR 465.2(a) makes it an unfair or deceptive act for a business "to write, create, or sell" a consumer review, consumer testimonial or celebrity testimonial that materially misrepresents that the reviewer or testimonialist exists, that they used or had experience with the product, service or business, or what that experience was. 465.2(b) covers disseminating a testimonial the business knew or should have known was fake or false. — [16 CFR 465.2 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/465.2)
- PRIMARY rule text: "consumer testimonial" is an advertising message consumers are likely to believe reflects the opinions, beliefs or experiences of a consumer who has purchased, used or otherwise had experience with a product, service or business; "testimonialist" is the individual "giving or purportedly giving" it; a required disclosure is "clear and conspicuous" only if it is "difficult to miss" and "unavoidable". — [16 CFR 465.1 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/465.1)
- PRIMARY FTC staff guidance: the rule "authorizes courts to impose civil penalties for knowing violations"; an "obviously fictional dramatization" is not a testimonial; using actors is not covered "unless the underlying testimonials were fake or false" but may still be deceptive under the FTC Act; insider testimonials (officers, managers, employees, agents and, for reviews, their immediate relatives) need a clear and conspicuous disclosure of the relationship unless it is otherwise clear to the audience; § 465.8 prohibits fake indicators of social media influence the business knew or should have known were fake. The guidance is described as not definitive or comprehensive. — [FTC, Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule: Questions and Answers](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)
- PRIMARY: on 22 December 2025 the FTC sent warning letters to 10 companies about possible violations of the rule and stated civil penalties of up to $53,088 per violation; this was the first public enforcement use of the rule (effective 21 October 2024). — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/12/ftc-warns-10-companies-about-possible-violations-agencys-new-consumer-review-rule)
- Law-firm commentary: each fake review or testimonial counts as a separate violation, and delegating review generation to a marketing agency without asking how it is done is not a defence under the "knew or should have known" standard. — [Benesch via JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/five-stars-zero-tolerance-ftc-turns-up-3594404/); [Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/the-ftc-cracks-down-on-fake-reviews-with-new-rule)
- Google's own guidance warns that seeking inauthentic mentions "isn't as helpful as it might seem" for AI search visibility, and structured data must reflect visible page content. — [Google Search Central, AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)

### Inferences
- Testimonials on a US-targeted home page that use invented names, stock headshots or made-up companies and read as real customer statements fit the text of 465.2(a) ("write, create" + "misrepresents ... that the ... testimonialist exists"). A small footnote is unlikely to cure this: the rule's disclosure standard is "unavoidable", and the "obviously fictional dramatization" carve-out requires the fiction to be obvious on its face.
- Unverifiable aggregate stats ("500+ clients", "98% satisfaction", "10x ROI") are not specifically addressed by Part 465, but they are ordinary advertising claims subject to general FTC Act deception and substantiation principles, and they also fail the "up-front disclosure" and "connection to the rest of the web" trust factors because a buyer cannot check them. Requirement candidate: every number on the home page must have a nameable source (own analytics, a named client, or a cited third-party study with a link).
- Proof stack for an agency with no famous logos, ordered by how checkable it is: links to third-party review profiles (Google Business Profile, Clutch, G2 or similar) with real counts; named testimonials with full name, role, company and ideally a link or photo used with permission; one or two detailed case write-ups with before/after figures and the measurement method; named, pictured founders with LinkedIn links; "from" prices or sample packages; a step-by-step process with timelines; plain compliance statements relevant to AI calling (for example call-recording consent and TCPA handling) and data handling; a concrete guarantee or pilot offer.
- Because 54% of software buyers talk to an existing user before buying, offering reference calls is a credible substitute for logo walls.

### Gaps
- The rule text refers to "a consumer" and does not define the term; the FTC Q&A page does not address business-to-business testimonials directly. Whether Part 465 penalties reach testimonials from business customers was not settled by the sources I read; general FTC Act section 5 deception law applies regardless. This needs a lawyer's confirmation.
- The FTC Q&A does not specifically address "illustrative" or "hypothetical" testimonials other than the "obviously fictional dramatization" statement.
- No study isolates which proof types work best for small or new B2B service vendors; the buyer surveys skew to mid-market and enterprise software.
- No quantitative research on the conversion effect of guarantees, founder visibility or compliance statements for agencies was found.

## 4. Interactive and explanatory components: ROI calculators, interactive demos, comparison tables, explanatory visuals

### Takeaway
Evidence that interactive components lift B2B conversion exists but is almost entirely vendor-produced; the best-supported direction is to let buyers experience the product themselves (demos and trials are among the most influential resources in buyer surveys). NN/g cautions that simple worked examples beat complex calculators for early-stage visitors.

### Cited Findings
- VENDOR (TrustRadius 2026): product demos and free trials rank among the most influential resources when buyers select a vendor. — [Demand Gen Report on TrustRadius 2026](https://www.demandgenreport.com/?p=53853)
- PRIMARY press release: 61% of B2B buyers prefer rep-free buying and favour self-service tools for general learning. — [Gartner](https://gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience)
- VENDOR (Navattic, 28,000+ demos on its own platform, 280 survey responses): top-quartile interactive demos average 50.1% engagement, 28.9% completion and 12.8% click-through; top 1% reach 84.4% engagement, 61.6% completion, 54.0% CTR; the vendor claims demo-to-opportunity conversion about 3x that of isolated product pages. SaaS product tours, not service agencies. — [Navattic, State of the Interactive Product Demo 2025](https://navattic.com/report/state-of-the-interactive-product-demo-2025); [Navattic benchmarks](https://www.navattic.com/blog/interactive-demos-industry-benchmarks)
- VENDOR: Navattic's 2024 report is cited as finding interactive demos increase website conversion by 16%. — [HockeyStack](https://hockeystack.com/lab-blog-posts/do-interactive-demos-work)
- UNVERIFIED, SECONDARY: an agency blog attributes to the Content Marketing Institute's B2B benchmark a conversion rate of 7.4% for interactive calculators vs 3.2% for static content (+131%); I could not confirm this in the original report. — [Brixon Group](https://brixongroup.com/en/?p=6529)
- OLD: in NN/g's B2B study a shipping calculator requiring ZIP codes, weights and dimensions was "much too complex and error prone" for an early-stage buyer, while a competitor's simple table of common prices was more useful; configurators suit highly engaged users only. — [NN/g, Show Prices for Common Scenarios](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/show-prices-for-common-scenarios/)
- NN/g: movement attracts attention automatically, so animated explanatory elements pull attention away from users' primary goals unless the motion itself carries the message. — [NN/g, Animation for Attention and Comprehension](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-usability/)

### Inferences
- For AI calling agents and chatbots the product can demonstrate itself: a "hear a sample call" audio player, a click-to-call demo number or a live chatbot answering questions about the visitor's own business type is the service-agency equivalent of an interactive product demo, and aligns with the self-service preference data. No study measures this specific pattern.
- If an ROI calculator is used, keep it to two or three inputs with pre-filled defaults and show the assumptions and their sources; also show two or three pre-computed scenarios in a simple table so visitors get the answer without interacting (per NN/g).
- A comparison table (for example "AI receptionist vs missed calls vs hiring staff", or package tiers) is consistent with NN/g's "simple table" finding, but I found no direct conversion evidence for comparison tables on B2B home pages.
- Interactive widgets should be user-initiated and lazy-loaded so they do not cost LCP or INP (see section 6).

### Gaps
- No independent or peer-reviewed study isolates the conversion effect of ROI calculators, comparison tables or explanatory visuals on B2B service sites.
- All interactive-demo benchmarks come from demo-software vendors measuring their own customers; metric definitions differ between vendors.

## 5. Motion and animation: scroll-driven animation, smooth-scroll libraries, parallax, autoplay

### Takeaway
Usability research finds that altering native scrolling causes more harm than benefit and that automatically moving content distracts and is missed; WCAG requires a pause/stop/hide control for any auto-starting motion longer than five seconds (Level A) and recommends that interaction-triggered motion can be disabled (AAA), with prefers-reduced-motion as a sufficient technique. Performance guidance restricts animation to transform and opacity; 40% of mobile pages already ship non-composited animations and median mobile Total Blocking Time is nearly two seconds.

### Cited Findings
- PRIMARY: NN/g's usability study of scrolljacking sites (including Apple, BBC News, Therabody) concluded "scrolljacking leads to more user pain than benefits"; "the majority of our study participants were at least mildly disoriented"; pages that altered scroll rate or direction while requiring reading "exhibited the most severe usability issues"; task-oriented users were annoyed and often left. Mitigations if used: keep it short, do not change direction, keep text minimal, alternate with normal scrolling, place it below the fold, keep sticky navigation visible, and avoid it on mobile. — [NN/g, Scrolljacking 101](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/)
- NN/g: motion in peripheral vision triggers an automatic shift of attention; before adding animation, define its goal, how often the user will see it and whether it is user-triggered; test participants called a repeated animation "nice the first time, but now it's getting annoying". — [NN/g, Animation for Attention and Comprehension](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-usability/)
- SECONDARY summary of NN/g's auto-forwarding carousel study: a user failed to find a target shown in 98-point type at the top of the page because the panel auto-rotated; auto-advancing content harms users with motor impairments, low literacy and screen-reader/keyboard users. — [Yale Usability](https://usability.yale.edu/usability-best-practices/hero-imagescarousels)
- PRIMARY: WCAG 2.2 SC 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide (Level A): for moving, blinking or scrolling information that starts automatically, lasts more than five seconds and is presented in parallel with other content, there must be a mechanism to pause, stop or hide it unless the motion is essential. Motion that starts on scrolling into view counts as automatic; auto-advancing carousels are "auto-updating" content with no five-second grace period; W3C recommends a single control that pauses all moving elements. — [W3C, Understanding SC 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)
- PRIMARY: WCAG 2.2 SC 2.3.3 Animation from Interactions (Level AAA): "Motion animation triggered by interaction can be disabled, unless the animation is essential". Parallax is named as typically non-essential; extra animation layered on scrolling must be disableable; honouring prefers-reduced-motion (techniques C39 and SCR40) is sufficient; vestibular reactions described include dizziness, nausea, headaches and migraine. — [W3C, Understanding SC 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)
- PRIMARY: Lenis documents its own limitations: no CSS scroll-snap support, "capped to 60fps on Safari" and "30fps on low power mode", smooth scroll stops over iframes, "position fixed seems to lag on MacOS Safari pre-M1", anchor links are prevented by default unless `anchors: true`, and nested scroll containers need configuration. It honours prefers-reduced-motion by default (smoothing disabled, programmatic scrolls jump); opting out via `respectReducedMotion: false` is "not recommended". — [Lenis README](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis)
- PRIMARY: "Where possible, restrict animations to opacity and transform to keep animations on the compositing stage"; "Avoid any property that triggers layout or paint unless it's absolutely necessary"; in web.dev's example a top/left animation dropped 50% of frames vs about 1% for the transform version; will-change should be used sparingly. — [web.dev, Animations guide](https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide)
- PRIMARY (July 2025 field/lab data): 40% of mobile pages and 44% of desktop pages have non-composited animations; median mobile Total Blocking Time is 1,916 ms (up 58% year on year) against a "good" lab threshold of 200 ms. — [HTTP Archive Web Almanac 2025, Performance](https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2025/performance)

### Inferences
- Requirement candidates: no scroll hijacking (no altered scroll speed or direction, no pinned multi-screen sequences containing essential text); every looping or auto-playing element longer than five seconds (logo marquees, ambient blobs, background video, auto-rotating testimonials) either stops within five seconds or has a visible pause control; all scroll-reveal, parallax and smooth-scroll effects are disabled under prefers-reduced-motion; animate only transform and opacity; no content is invisible until an animation runs (content must be readable with JavaScript delayed or motion disabled).
- A logo marquee needing a pause control is my application of SC 2.2.2 (W3C's own example is a stock ticker), not an example W3C lists.
- A smooth-scroll library adds a per-frame main-thread loop and has documented Safari and embedded-iframe limitations (relevant to embedded Cal.com and chat iframes); for a lead-generation home page the usability research gives no benefit to offset that cost. If retained, keep it desktop-only, keep default reduced-motion handling and enable anchor links.

### Gaps
- No published study quantifies the conversion or INP effect of Lenis or similar smooth-scroll libraries specifically; the performance concern is derived from general main-thread guidance and the library's own limitations list.
- I did not open NN/g's original auto-forwarding carousel article this session; findings are via university summaries.
- No prevalence figure for vestibular disorders appears in the W3C page; do not quote a percentage without a separate source.

## 6. Core Web Vitals in 2026: thresholds, pass rates, causes of poor LCP/INP on animation-heavy React sites, fixes

### Takeaway
Thresholds are unchanged (LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms, CLS ≤ 0.1 at the 75th percentile); only 48% of mobile origins pass all three and home pages do worse than interior pages (45% on mobile). The dominant failure is mobile LCP (62% good), usually an image that is discovered late, lazy-loaded or not prioritised, and INP problems trace to main-thread JavaScript: long tasks, client-side rendering, large DOMs and third-party widgets.

### Cited Findings
- PRIMARY: INP good ≤ 200 ms, poor > 500 ms, assessed at the 75th percentile of page loads segmented by mobile and desktop. — [web.dev, Optimize INP](https://web.dev/articles/optimize-inp)
- SECONDARY restatement: LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms, CLS ≤ 0.1 at the 75th percentile over 28 days of CrUX data; INP replaced FID in March 2024. — [Vercel guide](https://vercel.com/kb/guide/how-to-improve-core-web-vitals)
- PRIMARY (CrUX, July 2025): good CWV on 48% of mobile and 56% of desktop sites (mobile was 44% in 2024, 36% in 2023). Good LCP 62% mobile / 74% desktop; good INP 77% mobile / 97% desktop; good CLS 81% mobile / 72% desktop. — [Web Almanac 2025, Performance](https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2025/performance)
- PRIMARY: home pages pass CWV less often than secondary pages: 45% vs 56% on mobile and 47% vs 61% on desktop. — [Web Almanac 2025, Performance](https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2025/performance)
- PRIMARY: the LCP element is an image on 76% of mobile pages (text on 23.7%); only 17.3% of mobile pages use fetchpriority="high" on it and 2.1% preload it; about 16-17% of pages lazy-load their LCP image; LCP image formats are 57% JPG, 26% PNG, 11% WebP, 0.7% AVIF; good TTFB on only 44% of mobile sites; only 15% of mobile pages pass the render-blocking resources audit; 62% of mobile pages have unsized images; 87% use web fonts. — [Web Almanac 2025, Performance](https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2025/performance)
- PRIMARY: adding fetchpriority="high" to the LCP image improved LCP from 2.6 s to 1.9 s in a Google Flights test. — [web.dev, Fetch Priority](https://web.dev/articles/fetch-priority)
- PRIMARY: INP has three phases (input delay, processing duration, presentation delay). Causes and fixes: long tasks from script evaluation during load (reduce and split them); heavy event callbacks (do minimal work, yield to the main thread, update the UI first and defer the rest); layout thrashing (do not read layout right after writing styles); large DOMs ("large DOMs do require more work to render"; flatten, use content-visibility); client-side HTML rendering blocks the next frame (keep it small). — [web.dev, Optimize INP](https://web.dev/articles/optimize-inp)
- PRIMARY: "Chat widgets are another excellent use case for the facade technique. Most chat widgets download significant amounts of JavaScript that can negatively affect page load and responsiveness to user input." Pattern: render a static look-alike on load, preconnect on hover, swap in the real embed on click. — [web.dev, Lazy load third-party resources with facades](https://web.dev/third-party-facades)
- PRIMARY: use loading="lazy" for below-the-fold images and iframes, never for the LCP image. — [web.dev, Lazy load images and iframe elements](https://web.dev/learn/performance/lazy-load-images-and-iframe-elements)
- PRIMARY (2024 edition): when INP replaced FID, Next.js sites showed a 10% drop in CWV pass rate, among the largest declines; the authors point to heavy reliance on client-side rendering despite SSR/SSG being available, and caution the decline cannot be attributed solely to the technology. — [Web Almanac 2024, Performance](https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2024/performance)
- SECONDARY, 2024 data via Astro, unverified against the live dashboard: share of sites passing CWV was Astro 63%, WordPress 44%, Gatsby 42%, Next.js 27%, Nuxt 24%. — [dev.to summary](https://dev.to/mitrasish/astro-vs-nextjs-seo-core-web-vitals-and-the-catch-20hg); earlier [Astro 2023 report](https://astro.build/blog/2023-web-framework-performance-report) had Next.js at roughly 1 in 4
- WebAIM 2026: the average home page now has 1,437 DOM elements, up 14.3% in a year. — [WebAIM Million 2026](https://webaim.org/projects/million/)
- Google's AI-search guidance lists good page experience (cross-device display, low latency) among the things that matter for generative AI features too. — [Google Search Central, AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)

### Inferences
- Technical thresholds to set as acceptance criteria (field data, mobile, 75th percentile): LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms, CLS ≤ 0.1; lab guardrail TBT < 200 ms on a throttled mobile profile.
- For an animation-heavy Next.js marketing page the likely failure modes, mapped from the sources: (1) hero rendered or revealed by client JavaScript (opacity-0 until an animation fires, or text split into animated spans), which delays LCP; (2) hero image not prioritised or lazy-loaded; (3) whole-page "use client" trees that ship animation libraries in the initial bundle and inflate hydration long tasks; (4) chat and scheduling embeds loaded eagerly on every page; (5) per-frame scroll listeners and non-composited animations; (6) DOM bloat from decorative layers and split-text spans.
- Corresponding fixes: server-render the hero text so the LCP element is in the initial HTML and visible without JavaScript (text as LCP avoids image cost entirely); if the LCP is an image, give it priority/fetchpriority="high", explicit dimensions and a modern format; keep sections as server components and isolate interactivity in small client islands loaded with dynamic import; load chat and booking widgets through a facade on user intent or after idle; self-host fonts with size-adjusted fallbacks and a limited number of families/weights; animate transform/opacity only; cap DOM size; verify with field data, not only Lighthouse.
- Passing CWV is a differentiator for a home page specifically, since fewer than half of home pages pass on mobile.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve a current (2025-2026) per-framework pass rate for Next.js from the HTTP Archive Core Web Vitals Technology Report (the dashboard at https://httparchive.org/reports/cwv-tech did not render in my tools); the 27% figure is 2024 data via a competitor's summary.
- The 2025 Almanac performance chapter gives no quantitative third-party-script figures and no LCP sub-part breakdown for load delay or render delay.
- No source quantifies the specific LCP/INP cost of Cal.com or n8n chat embeds; the facade recommendation is generic.
- web.dev's font-loading guidance was not opened this session; the font recommendations above are inference from general LCP/CLS guidance.

## 7. Accessibility for a US-targeted marketing site in 2026: most-failed WCAG criteria, target size, focus, legal context

### Takeaway
95.9% of the top million home pages have automatically detectable WCAG failures and the same six error types account for almost all of them, led by low-contrast text on 83.9% of pages, so a home page that clears those six plus the WCAG 2.2 AA additions (24px targets, focus not obscured by sticky elements) is already far above the norm. Private-sector sites have no codified federal technical standard, but federal website-accessibility suits rose 27% in 2025 to 3,117 and WCAG 2.1/2.2 AA is the benchmark used in demand letters and settlements.

### Cited Findings
- PRIMARY (WebAIM Million 2026, data collected February 2026, automated testing of the top one million home pages): 95.9% had detected WCAG 2 failures (94.8% in 2025); average 56.1 errors per page (51 in 2025, +10.1%). — [WebAIM Million 2026](https://webaim.org/projects/million/)
- PRIMARY: most common failures, 2026 (2025 in brackets): low-contrast text 83.9% (79.1%); missing alternative text 53.1% (55.5%); missing form labels 51% (48.2%); empty links 46.3% (45.4%); empty buttons 30.6% (29.6%); missing document language 13.5% (15.8%). — [WebAIM Million 2026](https://webaim.org/projects/million/)
- PRIMARY: 82.7% of pages use ARIA and pages with ARIA average 59.1 errors vs 42 without; 41.8% of pages skip heading levels; 18.1% have more than one h1; 7.5% have no headings. Next.js pages (23,863 sampled) average 40.9 errors, 27.1% below the overall average; React pages average 43.5. Correlation, not causation. — [WebAIM Million 2026](https://webaim.org/projects/million/)
- Contrast threshold: WCAG requires 4.5:1 for body text. — [Priority Pixels on the 2026 WebAIM report](https://prioritypixels.co.uk/insights/web-accessibility-is-getting-worse-according-to-the-2026-webaim-million-report/)
- PRIMARY: WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum), Level AA: "The size of the target for pointer inputs is at least 24 by 24 CSS pixels", with exceptions for sufficient spacing (24px circles centred on each target must not intersect), an equivalent control on the same page, and inline links in text. — [W3C, Understanding SC 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)
- PRIMARY: WCAG 2.2 SC 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum), Level AA: a focused component "is not entirely hidden due to author-created content"; W3C's illustrative case is a sticky footer banner covering focused items. SC 2.4.13 Focus Appearance (AAA) describes a focus indicator at least as large as a 2 CSS pixel perimeter with 3:1 contrast between focused and unfocused states. SC 3.2.6 Consistent Help (A) requires help mechanisms such as a chat option to appear in the same relative order across pages. SC 3.3.7 Redundant Entry (A) and 2.5.7 Dragging Movements (AA) are also new. — [W3C, What's New in WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/)
- PRIMARY: SC 2.2.2 (Level A) requires pause/stop/hide for auto-starting motion over five seconds. — [W3C, Understanding SC 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)
- SECONDARY (Seyfarth Shaw counts): federal website-accessibility lawsuits under ADA Title III reached 3,117 in 2025, up 27% from 2,452 in 2024 and the highest since the 2022 peak of 3,255; they were 36% of all 8,667 federal Title III filings (28% in 2024); monthly filings ranged from 219 to 319. Seyfarth notes many more demand letters resolve privately, so filings understate exposure. — [JD Supra, Federal Court Website Accessibility Lawsuit Filings Bounce Back in 2025](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/federal-court-website-accessibility-1182174/)
- SECONDARY, internally inconsistent: UsableNet's count including key state courts is reported as roughly 5,000 digital accessibility suits in 2025 (4,928 in its tracker; "more than 5,000" in its blog; one analysis gives 3,195 federal + 1,919 state), with 1,427 against companies that had already been sued. — [ADA Compliance Pros analysis](https://www.adacompliancepros.com/blog/who-gets-sued-web-accessibility-filing-data)
- DOJ's 2024 Title II rule adopts WCAG 2.1 AA for state and local governments; an interim final rule of 20 April 2026 moved compliance dates to 26 April 2027 (population 50,000+) and 26 April 2028 (smaller entities). It does not apply to private businesses. — [JD Supra, DOJ Extends Title II Deadlines](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/doj-extends-title-ii-ada-web-2176958/)
- Title III has no DOJ regulation specifying a WCAG version for private business websites; courts have widely applied the ADA to business websites, and WCAG 2.1 AA is the standard most consistently referenced in demand letters, settlements and consent decrees. — [Bureau of Internet Accessibility](https://www.boia.org/blog/is-wcag-required-by-the-ada); [Accessible.org](https://accessible.org/is-ada-compliance-mandatory-for-websites/)

### Inferences
- Acceptance checklist derived from the failure data: text contrast ≥ 4.5:1 (≥ 3:1 for large text) including text over gradients, glass panels and images, and including placeholder, muted and orange-on-white accent text; meaningful alt text on informative images and empty alt on decorative ones; a programmatic label on every form control; an accessible name on every icon-only link and button (social icons, menu toggle, carousel arrows, floating booking and chat buttons); lang attribute on html; exactly one h1 and no skipped heading levels; all pointer targets ≥ 24x24 CSS px (aim for 44-48 px on mobile, see section 9); visible focus indicators that are not hidden behind the sticky header, floating booking button or chat launcher; pause control for moving content; reduced-motion support.
- Glassmorphism, gradient text and low-opacity body copy are the design patterns most likely to produce the number-one failure (contrast), so they need explicit contrast checks in both resting and hover states.
- Third-party embeds (chat widget, scheduling iframe) inherit the site owner's accessibility exposure; their launchers need accessible names and keyboard operability.
- Automated tests catch only a subset of issues, so a keyboard-only pass and a screen-reader pass of the home page should be part of acceptance.

### Gaps
- Seyfarth's original post (adatitleiii.com) returned HTTP 403; figures are via JD Supra's republication. State-by-state counts and plaintiff-firm concentration were not retrieved.
- UsableNet's primary year-end PDF was not opened; totals conflict across write-ups.
- I found no data on how often small B2B service agencies specifically (as opposed to e-commerce and restaurants) are targeted.
- Whether DOJ finalised the April 2026 interim rule after the June 2026 comment period was not confirmed.

## 8. Search and AI-search visibility for a home page in 2026

### Takeaway
Google's May 2026 guidance says generative AI features run on the same ranking systems as Search and need no special files, schema, chunking or "AI writing"; what matters is indexable, crawlable, unique first-hand content and a good page experience. Independent studies agree that llms.txt and added schema show no measurable effect on AI citations, while off-site brand mentions correlate most strongly with AI visibility and passages containing statistics, quotations and cited sources are more likely to be surfaced.

### Cited Findings
- PRIMARY: "The best practices for SEO continue to be relevant" for generative AI features because they are built on Google's core ranking and quality systems; pages must be indexed and snippet-eligible; JavaScript sites should follow standard JavaScript SEO practice; Google calls unique, non-commodity content with first-hand experience the factor most likely to influence AI visibility over time. — [Google Search Central, Optimizing for generative AI search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)
- PRIMARY, what is not needed: Google Search "doesn't use" llms.txt or other AI-specific files; "There's no requirement to break your content into tiny pieces for AI to better understand it"; "You don't need to write in a specific way just for generative AI search"; "there's no special schema.org markup you need to add"; structured data "isn't required for generative AI search" but remains worthwhile for rich-result eligibility; "AEO" and "GEO" are described as SEO; mass-producing pages to manipulate AI responses can violate the scaled content abuse policy. — [Google Search Central, Optimizing for generative AI search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide); announced in [Search Central Blog, May 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/05/a-new-resource-for-optimizing)
- PRIMARY: Google recommends placing Organization markup on the home page (or a single about page), not every page; no property is required; recommended properties include name, alternateName, legalName, description, url, logo (at least 112x112 px, crawlable), sameAs, contactPoint, email, telephone, address, foundingDate, numberOfEmployees and identifiers; use the most specific applicable subtype; the markup helps Google disambiguate the organization and can influence the knowledge panel. — [Google Search Central, Organization structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/organization)
- PRIMARY: since August 2023 FAQ rich results are shown only for well-known, authoritative government and health sites; other sites need not remove the markup. — [Google Search Central Blog](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/08/howto-faq-changes)
- VENDOR with disclosed method (SE Ranking, about 300,000 domains, November 2025): 10.13% of domains had an llms.txt file; neither statistical analysis nor an XGBoost model found any effect on how often a domain is cited by LLMs, and removing the variable improved model accuracy. Correlational. — [SE Ranking](https://seranking.com/blog/llms-txt/); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/llms-txt-shows-no-clear-effect-on-ai-citations-based-on-300k-domains/561542/)
- VENDOR, controlled design (Ahrefs, 2026): 1,885 pages that added JSON-LD between August 2025 and March 2026, matched against 4,000 control pages, showed no meaningful citation uplift on any AI platform (AI Overview citations were about 4.6% lower relative to controls). Limitation: every page already had 100+ AI Overview citations, so it does not test whether schema helps a page get noticed initially. — [Ahrefs, schema and AI citations](https://ahrefs.com/blog/schema-ai-citations/)
- VENDOR, correlational (Ahrefs, 75,000 brands): correlation with AI Overview brand mentions was 0.664 for branded web mentions, 0.527 for branded anchors, 0.392 for branded search volume and 0.218 for backlinks; about 26% of brands had zero mentions. — [Ahrefs, AI Overview brand visibility factors](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/)
- VENDOR: in July 2025 Ahrefs found 76.1% of AI Overview-cited pages ranked in the organic top 10 (1.9M citations); a March 2026 update (863,000 keywords, 4M URLs) is reported at 38% top 10, 31.2% positions 11-100 and 31.0% beyond 100, with Ahrefs attributing part of the change to improved citation detection. — [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/search-rankings-ai-citations); [DesignRush summary](https://news.designrush.com/ai-overview-citations-drop-ahrefs)
- PEER-REVIEWED (KDD 2024; Princeton, Georgia Tech, IIT Delhi, Allen Institute; 10,000 queries on a Bing Chat-like engine): adding statistics, quotations and cited sources raised source visibility by up to about 40% (41% on position-adjusted word count, 28% on subjective impression); on Perplexity (200-query subset) quotation addition gave +22% and statistics addition about +37% on subjective impression; keyword stuffing reduced visibility by roughly 9-10%. Limitations: synthetic evaluation, 2023-2024 models, gains are maxima and largest for lower-ranked sources. — [GEO: Generative Engine Optimization (arXiv 2311.09735)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.09735)
- SMALL SAMPLE (15 domains, blog posts): 72.4% of blog posts cited by ChatGPT contained an "answer capsule" (a short self-contained answer directly under a question-style heading); capsules without links correlated with more referrals. — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/how-to-get-cited-by-chatgpt-the-content-traits-llms-quote-most-464868)
- WebAIM 2026: 18.1% of home pages have more than one h1 and 7.5% have none. — [WebAIM Million 2026](https://webaim.org/projects/million/)

### Inferences
- Home-page requirements: one descriptive h1 containing the service category and audience in visible, server-rendered text; a title of the form "brand + what it does + for whom"; the core description, services, FAQ answers and proof present in the initial HTML rather than injected after hydration or hidden behind animation states; Organization JSON-LD on the home page with consistent name, logo, URL, contact, address, founding date and sameAs links to real profiles, matching the visible content and the name/address/phone used elsewhere on the web.
- E-E-A-T for an agency translates into verifiable entity signals: named founders with credentials and profile links, a real address and phone that match business listings, first-hand case evidence, and third-party mentions and reviews. The Ahrefs correlations suggest off-site brand mentions matter more for AI visibility than anything on the home page itself.
- Writing for citation: lead each section with a one or two sentence direct answer, include specific figures with a named source and link, and keep FAQ answers self-contained. This is supported by the GEO paper and the answer-capsule observation; Google says no special writing style is required, so treat it as good plain writing rather than a ranking technique. FAQ content should be written for users; FAQPage markup will not produce a rich result for an agency site and showed no citation benefit in the Ahrefs study.
- llms.txt: optional, low cost, no demonstrated benefit; Google ignores it. Not a redesign priority.
- Fabricated statistics or testimonials are doubly risky here: they are the kind of "inauthentic" signal Google says does not help, and AI systems that surface them propagate unverifiable claims under the brand's name.

### Gaps
- Google's guidance does not prescribe home-page title or h1 formats; the title/h1 recommendations above are inference plus general SEO practice. I did not open Google's title-link or E-E-A-T (helpful content) documentation this session.
- Evidence on what gets cited by ChatGPT and Perplexity is vendor-produced and correlational; the one peer-reviewed study used 2023-2024 systems.
- The content-freshness claims circulating (for example "2.8x more citations for content under six months old") come from vendor posts without disclosed methods and were not verified.
- No study addresses AI-search citation of small local/regional service agencies specifically.

## 9. Mobile: share of B2B traffic, conversion, thumb reach and tap targets

### Takeaway
Reliable B2B-specific device splits are scarce: third-party figures put mobile at half or more of B2B visits while desktop converts at roughly 1.7-2x the mobile rate, so the mobile home page is where most first impressions happen even if conversions complete on desktop. Mobile is also where performance is worst (48% CWV pass, 77% good INP), and tap-target guidance converges on 24 px as the WCAG AA floor and 44-48 px as the platform recommendation.

### Cited Findings
- SECONDARY, single underlying dataset attributed variously to Dynamic Yield and WordStream: mobile is reported at over 60% of B2B website visits, with average conversion 2.49% on mobile vs 5.06% on desktop. — [Discovered Labs, February 2026](https://discoveredlabs.com/blog/mobile-conversion-rate-optimization-a-playbook-for-marketing-leaders)
- VENDOR blog, source not traceable: in B2B SaaS, 70%+ of paid conversions happen on desktop even where 50%+ of traffic is mobile. — [Sleek Analytics, May 2026](https://www.getsleek.io/blog/mobile-vs-desktop-analytics-2026)
- All-vertical landing-page survey (not B2B-specific): mobile conversion averaged 3.84% vs 6.42% on desktop in 2026, with the gap narrowing since 2022. — [Visionary Marketing 2026](https://visionary-marketing.co.uk/blog/conversion-benchmark-report-2026)
- PRIMARY: on mobile, 48% of sites pass CWV, 62% have good LCP, 77% good INP; median mobile TBT is 1,916 ms vs 92 ms on desktop. — [Web Almanac 2025, Performance](https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2025/performance)
- OLD (Google/SOASTA 2016-2017): 53% of mobile site visits are abandoned if a page takes longer than 3 seconds to load; bounce probability rises 113% as load time goes from 1 s to 7 s. — [Think with Google benchmarks PDF](https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/_qs/documents/1632/au-mobile-page-speed-new-industry-benchmarks.pdf); [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/google-53-of-mobile-users-abandon-sites-that-take-over-3-seconds-to-load/426070/)
- OLD (2013 field observation, 1,333 people of whom 780 were touching the screen): 49% one-handed, 36% cradled, 15% two-handed; later commentary cautions that grips vary and the "thumb zone" should not be over-relied on. — [Smashing Magazine, The Thumb Zone](https://smashingmagazine.com/2016/09/the-thumb-zone-designing-for-mobile-users)
- PRIMARY: Google's Android accessibility guidance recommends touch targets of at least 48x48 dp (about 9 mm) separated by 8 dp or more; the visible icon can be smaller if padding makes up the target. — [Google Android Accessibility Help](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/7101858)
- PRIMARY: WCAG 2.2 AA minimum is 24x24 CSS px with a spacing exception. — [W3C, Understanding SC 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)
- PRIMARY: NN/g advises against scrolljacking on mobile in particular (longer sequences, slower loading, collapsed navigation makes escape harder). — [NN/g, Scrolljacking 101](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/)
- Single-site test: a sticky CTA at the bottom of the mobile viewport outperformed a top placement by about 7% in conversion rate, with a revenue-per-visitor caveat flagged by the agency. — [Blend Commerce](https://blendcommerce.com/blogs/ab-tests-shopify/better-placed-sticky-add-to-cart-product-page-conversion)

### Inferences
- Design mobile first for comprehension and trust (headline, one CTA, proof visible in the first screenful at roughly 360-390 px wide), and expect a meaningful share of conversions to finish on desktop; make the mobile CTA low-commitment (tap-to-call, short form, or booking) and measure device paths in analytics.
- Tap targets: 24x24 px is the compliance floor; use 44-48 px height for primary buttons, nav toggles, FAQ accordion headers and floating launchers, with at least 8 px between adjacent targets.
- Floating elements compete for the bottom of a small screen: a chat launcher, a booking button and a sticky CTA bar together can obscure content and focused elements (SC 2.4.11) and crowd the thumb-reach area; consolidate to one persistent mobile action.
- Disable smooth-scroll, parallax and pinned scroll sequences on touch devices, and budget JavaScript against mid-range Android hardware, since mobile TBT is roughly 20x desktop.

### Gaps
- No authoritative, openly published 2025-2026 benchmark of mobile traffic share for B2B service or agency sites was found; Contentsquare's B2B cut is gated, and the figures above are secondary and likely SaaS-skewed. The site's own analytics should be used instead.
- No recent (post-2017) replication of the 53% abandonment figure or of the 2013 one-handed-use observation was found.
- No mobile-specific conversion research for B2B lead forms versus booking calendars was found.
