# Competitor home page benchmark: AI voice-agent products and AI automation agencies (fetched 2026-10-08)

Method note for the report writer. Every page below was fetched live on 2026-10-08 and read as extracted page text through a summarising fetch tool. No screenshots were taken, so anything that depends on seeing the page (colour scheme, typeface, motion, hero imagery) is NOT verified unless the page text itself states it. Headlines and CTA labels are quoted as the extraction returned them. Pages covered: 10 voice products/services (Synthflow, Bland, Retell AI, Vapi, Goodcall, Smith.ai, Dialzara, Frontdesk AI / My AI Front Desk, Rosie, Upfirst), 1 human-plus-AI receptionist service (Ruby), 3 agencies (Morningside AI, AutomateNexus, OptiWork.ai). Not usable: air.ai (the domain now serves an unrelated defense-readiness company), poweredby.agency (HTTP 403), play.ai (not attempted). TinyFish tools were not authenticated in this session, so WebFetch/WebSearch were used.

## 1. For each site: hero headline, sub-headline, CTAs, hero visual, and ordered sections

### Takeaway
SMB-facing voice products lead with "never miss a call" outcomes plus a free trial and put a way to hear the agent within the first screen or two; enterprise platforms lead with risk/compliance and a booked demo. The three agencies lead with the buyer's pain or the price of removing it, not with "AI", and two of the three publish prices on the home page.

## 2. How AI voice products let a visitor experience the product on the home page, and which methods are realistic for a small agency

### Takeaway
Nine of the eleven voice/receptionist pages offer some way to hear or talk to the agent from the home page, and the methods form a ladder of effort: published demo phone line, pre-recorded audio samples, in-browser talk widget, and a "call me" form. The demo phone line and recorded samples are the lowest-effort rungs and are used by the SMB-focused brands themselves.

### Cited Findings
- Call-me form: Retell's hero asks for a use case, name and phone number and promises "Receive a live call from our agent", submitted with "Get a call". — [Retell AI](https://www.retellai.com)
- In-browser talk widget: Vapi's hero has a "Start call" button with three use-case tabs and a "Mic permissions needed" notice. — [Vapi](https://vapi.ai)
- In-browser talk widget with industry choice: Bland's hero reads "Click to speak with an agent" with Bland, Healthcare, Insurance and Financial services options. — [Bland](https://www.bland.ai)
- Published demo line: Vapi "Hear for yourself" 1-844-HEY-VAPI; Dialzara "Try the Live Demo" / "Hear it live right now." 866-545-ZARA with "Tap to call"; Smith.ai "Live demo — call (380) 333-6907"; Frontdesk lists a demo line "+1 (864) 619-0619" in its FAQ; Ruby "Hear the Ruby difference for yourself. Call us: 844-311-7829". — [Vapi](https://vapi.ai); [Dialzara](https://dialzara.com); [Smith.ai](https://smith.ai); [Frontdesk AI](https://www.myaifrontdesk.com); [Ruby](https://www.ruby.com)
- Recorded call samples: Upfirst's "Listen to how Upfirst handles real calls." has six clips with durations (for example "Listen · 1:12"), each paired with a short feature description. — [Upfirst](https://upfirst.ai)
- Recorded call samples by scenario: Rosie's "Hear Rosie in Action..." offers four call scenarios. — [Rosie](https://heyrosie.com)
- Audio by industry: Synthflow shows eight vertical cards each with a "Hear Demo" button (the extraction reported the buttons linking to a placeholder anchor). — [Synthflow](https://synthflow.ai)
- Voice picker: Goodcall's "Choose a voice that fits your business." has "Hear Rosie", "Hear Alex" and "Hear Hero". — [Goodcall](https://www.goodcall.com)
- Scripted animation instead of live audio: Goodcall's hero plays "Rosie's Plumbing Example", simulating setup, a sample call and a booked appointment, with a pause control. — [Goodcall](https://www.goodcall.com)
- Product video: Frontdesk's hero video shows the receptionist "answering an inbound call, qualifying the lead, and booking an appointment 24/7." — [Frontdesk AI](https://www.myaifrontdesk.com)
- Text transcript by industry: Upfirst's "Upfirst is for." pairs each of four industries with a sample caller exchange. — [Upfirst](https://upfirst.ai)
- Website-first onboarding as the hero action: Frontdesk's hero field asks for "Your business website" with the button "Get My AI Worker →". — [Frontdesk AI](https://www.myaifrontdesk.com)
- No experience element found in page text: Synthflow's hero and Morningside, AutomateNexus and OptiWork (none of the three agencies has audio, a demo or a chat widget on the home page). — [Synthflow](https://synthflow.ai); [Morningside AI](https://www.morningside.ai); [AutomateNexus](https://automatenexus.com); [OptiWork.ai](https://optiwork.ai)

### Inferences
- Realism ranking for a small agency, lowest effort first: (1) a published demo phone number with tap-to-call, which only needs one configured agent; (2) three to six recorded sample calls labelled by industry with durations, which need no live infrastructure; (3) a scripted transcript or call-flow animation, which Syenxa's existing framer-motion/GSAP stack can produce; (4) an in-browser talk widget; (5) a call-me form. This ranking is my judgment from the patterns above, not something any source states.
- A call-me form is the riskiest option for a small US-facing agency because it initiates an outbound AI call to a submitted number, which raises consent questions; Retell itself names TCPA as a regulatory framework in its FAQ. No source in this research gives legal guidance, so this should be checked before building.
- None of the three agency pages lets a visitor hear an agent. An agency home page with a working demo line or audio samples would be showing something its direct agency peers do not.
- Pairing each sample with an industry label matters as much as the audio itself: Synthflow, Bland, Vapi, Rosie and Upfirst all key the demo to a vertical or use case.

### Gaps
- Whether the Bland and Vapi widgets and Synthflow "Hear Demo" buttons work without sign-up was not tested (read-only constraint, and no browser session was used).
- No source gave conversion data comparing these demo types; a search for 2026 teardowns of voice-agent landing pages returned only templates and agency pitches. — [Framer CAPSE template listing](https://www.framer.com/community/marketplace/templates/capse/)
- The cost and build effort of embedding a third-party web-call widget was not researched.

### Inferences
- A small agency without enterprise logos has three proven substitutes on these pages: named owner testimonials with business name and title (Upfirst, Rosie), a founder-credential block with photo (OptiWork), and platform-partner or tool badges (AutomateNexus).
- For SMB buyers, one concrete number per customer (Frontdesk's card pattern) reads as more credible than a wall of aggregate stats, and it is achievable with three or four clients.
- Compliance badges are not table stakes for SMB receptionist pages, but HIPAA is the one that SMB-facing brands do mention, which fits the dental and medical verticals these products chase.
- Thin agency proof is the most exploitable gap: two of the three agencies fetched show no client evidence at all on the home page.

### Gaps
- Retell's customer logos are images without readable names; file names suggested CVS, SelectQuote and Morgan & Morgan but this is unconfirmed.
- Whether any quoted customer metrics are independently verified is unknown; all are vendor-reported.
- Dialzara's three statistic blocks and its testimonial section rendered as "0%" placeholders and empty in the extracted text, so their real content is unknown.

## 4. How agencies present multiple services on one home page without looking generic

### Takeaway
The agencies that avoid the template look do not show a grid of equal service cards. They organise services around a sequence (Morningside's Identify, Develop, Adopt), a priced menu (AutomateNexus), or a best-fit description per service (OptiWork), and two of the three publish starting prices on the home page.

### Cited Findings
- Services as a three-step model: Morningside presents Identify, Develop and Adopt as three stacked cards under "Our days consist of three things...", each with a "View service" link; no tabs, bento grid or industry selector. — [Morningside AI](https://www.morningside.ai)
- Services as a priced menu: AutomateNexus's "A short menu. Every line has a price." groups eight offers under "Start here", "Fixed-price setups", "Builds" and "Afterwards" (Automation audit, AI workspace rollout, Grok Bot pilot, Speed-to-lead system, Paper-to-paid system, Private AI workspace, Systems connector, Care plan). — [AutomateNexus](https://automatenexus.com)
- Price in the hero: AutomateNexus's hero panel is a "Schedule of fees" (free audit, fixed-price setup from $3,500, fixed-scope build from $7,500, care plan $350/month). — [AutomateNexus](https://automatenexus.com)
- Outcome-named packages: AutomateNexus names services after the result ("Speed-to-lead system", "Paper-to-paid system") rather than the technology. — [AutomateNexus](https://automatenexus.com)
- Multiple entry points by audience: AutomateNexus has both an industry selector ("Where AI automation pays first", six cards) and a role grid ("Every team pays its own tax."). — [AutomateNexus](https://automatenexus.com)
- Services with a "Best fit" line: OptiWork lists four services (AI Consulting & Advisory, Business Process Automation, Custom AI Agents, Ongoing Support) as stacked text blocks, each with a description, a "Best fit" line and a "Learn about" link. — [OptiWork.ai](https://optiwork.ai)
- Price signals on the home page: OptiWork states a fixed $4,500 roadmap credited toward a build within 90 days, process automation "from $12,000", custom agents "from $20,000" and support "from $500 a month". — [OptiWork.ai](https://optiwork.ai)

- ROI calculator on the home page: OptiWork places "Calculate Your Automation ROI" directly under the hero and repeats it lower down. — [OptiWork.ai](https://optiwork.ai)
- Explicit positioning against other agencies: AutomateNexus's "Partner, not vendor." compares itself with "Other agencies". — [AutomateNexus](https://automatenexus.com)
- No pricing: Morningside shows no prices, packages or free audit. — [Morningside AI](https://www.morningside.ai)

- Free entry offers: AutomateNexus "free audit" with a written plan and a "$500 Pilot" nav link; OptiWork "free Shortlist Call"; Smith.ai "25 calls / month, free forever". — [AutomateNexus](https://automatenexus.com); [OptiWork.ai](https://optiwork.ai); [Smith.ai](https://smith.ai)

### Inferences
- Dialzara's structure is the most transferable to Syenxa's four-service mix: lead with one flagship offer in full depth (the AI calling agent), then introduce the other services in a compact "more than X" band, rather than giving four services equal weight above the fold.
- Naming services by outcome ("Speed-to-lead system") and attaching a "from" price or a "best fit" line are the two cheapest ways to stop a service section reading as a template.
- A single connecting narrative (one enquiry flowing from website to chatbot to call to booking to CRM) would let web development and digital marketing appear as parts of the same lead pipeline as the AI agents, in the way AutomateNexus's "One enquiry, no retyping." pipeline does. This framing is my suggestion; no fetched agency combines web/marketing with AI in this way.
- Published prices are an agency differentiator in their own right: the SMB products all show prices, and the two SMB-focused agencies do too, while the consultancy-style agency does not.

### Gaps
- No fetched agency sells website development and digital marketing alongside AI agents, so there is no direct precedent for Syenxa's exact service mix; a targeted search for web-plus-AI agencies was not completed.
- Only three agency home pages were read; the sample is too small to call any agency pattern dominant.
- Whether bento grids are used on any of these pages could not be determined from page text.

## 6. Visual design trends: light vs dark, typography, colour, motion, density, and what looks dated

### Takeaway
This question is only partly answered. Page text shows a shift toward editorial devices (italic emphasis words, figure labels, price tables as hero graphics) and away from abstract hero art, and one credible 2026 trade review says blob heroes, heavy glassmorphism and 3D heroes rarely ship on conversion pages. Light versus dark, fonts and colour on the benchmarked pages were not observed.

### Cited Findings
- A May 2026 review by a web studio (updated 30 September 2026) describes itself as "an editorial assessment, not a representative market study" and bases its claims on the studio's own projects. — [StudioMeyer](https://studiomeyer.io/en/blog/webdesign-trends-2026-reality-check)
- The same review says organic blobs and asymmetric containers "almost never ship on B2B SaaS, e-commerce or any conversion-critical flow." — [StudioMeyer](https://studiomeyer.io/en/blog/webdesign-trends-2026-reality-check)

- It says kinetic typography is acceptable on the hero only, must remain readable without animation, and that split text must not break screen readers or crawlers. — [StudioMeyer](https://studiomeyer.io/en/blog/webdesign-trends-2026-reality-check)
- It says 3D/WebGL is justified only for product viewers and configurators with an accessible fallback. — [StudioMeyer](https://studiomeyer.io/en/blog/webdesign-trends-2026-reality-check)

- Hero visuals named in page text are concrete rather than abstract: a talk widget (Vapi, Bland), a call form (Retell), a tap-to-call card (Dialzara), a labelled example animation (Goodcall), a dashboard screenshot (Upfirst), a product video (Frontdesk), a fee schedule (AutomateNexus). — [Vapi](https://vapi.ai); [Bland](https://www.bland.ai); [Retell AI](https://www.retellai.com); [Dialzara](https://dialzara.com); [Goodcall](https://www.goodcall.com); [Upfirst](https://upfirst.ai); [Frontdesk AI](https://www.myaifrontdesk.com); [AutomateNexus](https://automatenexus.com)

- Density varies widely among SMB pages: Rosie runs about 21 sections including full pricing and a 16-question FAQ, Upfirst about 10 with an 8-question FAQ. — [Rosie](https://heyrosie.com); [Upfirst](https://upfirst.ai)
- Count-up statistics can fail to render for non-browser readers: Dialzara's three statistic blocks and its "Missed calls" counter appeared as "0%" and "0" in the extracted page text. — [Dialzara](https://dialzara.com)
- Template marketplaces now sell "AI voice agent landing page" kits built around a hero with a live voice-call UI followed by problem, product, case study, process and FAQ sections. — [Framer CAPSE template listing](https://www.framer.com/community/marketplace/templates/capse/)

### Inferences
- The dated set for this category is likely: abstract gradient-blob heroes, glass panels used as the main card style, generic waveform art, and unlabelled dashboard mockups. This is supported by one trade review plus the absence of abstract hero art in the page text of the benchmarked sites; it is not confirmed by visual inspection.
- Syenxa's current stack leans on patterns the review flags (project notes list a `.glass-panel` utility, a `GlassOrchestra.jsx` of "ambient background glows and floating glass blobs", and a word-by-word `FluidHeader.jsx` reveal). These are the elements most at risk of reading as dated or generic; that reading is my inference from the project documentation, not from viewing the live site.
- The Dialzara "0%" result is a direct warning for Syenxa's `countup.jsx`: numbers animated from zero on the client can be read as zero by crawlers and AI readers. Server-rendering the final value and animating as an enhancement avoids this. I did not test Syenxa's own page.
- Because voice-agent landing pages are now a commodity template, the hero-widget-plus-feature-grid layout alone will not differentiate; the specific example business, prices and real audio do.

### Gaps
- Light versus dark theme, typefaces, accent colours, illustration style and actual motion on all fourteen pages were NOT observed. A screenshot pass (desktop and mobile) of at least Goodcall, Upfirst, Rosie, Bland, Vapi and AutomateNexus is needed to answer this question properly.
- Design galleries (Land-book, saaslandingpage.com, Awwwards, Lapa Ninja, Godly) were not consulted.
- Only one design-trend source was read in full, and it is a studio blog with a commercial interest and no stated sample size. Other trend articles surfaced in search were not opened and are not relied on here.

### Inferences
- Gaps a small agency can exploit, in order of how well the evidence supports them: (1) let visitors hear an agent, which no fetched agency does; (2) show real client proof, which two of three agencies lack; (3) publish "from" prices and a fixed-scope setup, which the SMB products and two agencies do but most agencies reportedly avoid; (4) offer done-for-you setup and ongoing management as the answer to the self-serve products' "15 minutes" pitch, since the SMB products assume the owner configures the agent; (5) embed a missed-call calculator, which no product page embeds; (6) add an AI versus receptionist versus voicemail comparison table, which no page has.
- The self-serve products set a price anchor of roughly $25-$80 per month. An agency charging setup fees has to justify the difference on the page, and the justification available from these patterns is managed setup, integration into existing tools, and a combined pipeline across site, chat, calls and follow-up.
- The most transferable FAQ set for Syenxa's home page is the SMB one: keep my number, after-hours only, human handoff, will callers know, setup time, what it costs, cancel anytime, data security, plus the agency-specific "who maintains it" and "do we own it".
- Leading with one number the visitor can check against their own business (calls missed, response time, cost per call) fits the category better than abstract capability claims; every SMB page reviewed does this.

### Gaps
- None of the vendor statistics (for example Smith.ai's "67%" first-responder figure) was traced to an original study; treat them as vendor claims.
- No independent data was found on which headline framing converts best for SMB voice-agent pages.
- Syenxa Tech's own current home page was not fetched in this research, so the gap list is derived from competitor patterns and the project's documentation rather than a side-by-side comparison.
